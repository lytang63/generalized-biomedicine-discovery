Generalized Biomedicine Discovery

Cognitive-Inspired Open-World Discovery for Medical Imaging

Luyao Tang¹, Yingkai Yang², Hanqi Chen², Jiewei Zheng³, Chaoqi Chen², Cheng Chen†¹

¹ The University of Hong Kong · ² Shenzhen University · ³ Xiamen University

📄Paper ⌘Code 🖼️Poster { }BibTeX

In real-world clinical practice, medical images face open-world shifts: long-tailed rare diseases, subtle lesions dominated by normal anatomy, and hierarchical taxonomies. Yet most open-world paradigms assume flat, balanced label spaces. We introduce GBD, a unified benchmark spanning three clinically grounded discovery settings, and SCAN, a cognitive-inspired method that exposes novelty suppressed by dominant visual manifolds.

🏥Three Clinical SettingsLong-tail rare disease, normal-to-abnormal, and within-taxonomy discovery.
💡Dominant-Pattern InsightRecurring structure forms a visual manifold that masks subtle novelty.
🧠Cognition-Inspired SCANPredictive suppression, surprise salience, and complementary accommodation.

TL;DR

Medical discovery is open-world in three ways at once: rare diseases in the long tail, tiny lesions surrounded by normal anatomy, and unseen subtypes within diagnostic families. GBD provides the first unified benchmark for these realistic biomedical shifts. SCAN addresses the shared failure mode—dominant known patterns suppress weak novelty signals—through a three-stage cognitive progression that works across all settings without auxiliary losses or anomaly labels.

💡 Core insight

Dominant known patterns form a visual manifold that masks subtle novelty.

When recurring structure—frequent diseases, normal anatomy, or parent-level morphology—dominates the embedding, weak evidence for rare diseases, subtle lesions, or new sibling subtypes is attenuated by global representation learning. Discovery requires exposing these suppressed signals without prior knowledge of what they contain.

🔬 Three Clinical Settings

Unified protocol, different clinical challenges.

All three settings share the same GCD protocol—only known classes are labeled, while unlabeled data mixes known and novel samples. What changes is the clinical source of the partition and, consequently, the visual evidence SCAN must protect or expose.

📊

Setting I: Long-tail Rare Disease Discovery

Clinical Motivation: Annotations focus on frequent conditions. Rare diseases in the long tail are clinically critical but sparsely observed.

Dominant-Pattern Problem: Frequent head-class patterns dominate the embedding, burying low-frequency cues from rare tail diseases.

SCAN's Strategy: Build a head-pattern anchor, then geometrically isolate tail-specific residuals.

🔍

Setting II: Normal-to-Abnormal Discovery

Clinical Motivation: Clinicians detect pathology relative to diverse normal baselines. Multiple normal anatomical variants are labeled; abnormal findings must be discovered.

Dominant-Pattern Problem: Normal anatomy occupies most pixels, leaving subtle lesions underrepresented in the feature space.

SCAN's Strategy: Suppress the normal-anatomy manifold, then amplify localized abnormal residuals using surprise-evoked salience.

🌳

Setting III: Within-Taxonomy Discovery

Clinical Motivation: Medical taxonomies are hierarchical. New subtypes emerge as fine-grained siblings within established diagnostic families.

Dominant-Pattern Problem: Semantically adjacent siblings share most morphological context, making fine-grained subtype differences hard to isolate.

SCAN's Strategy: Preserve parent-level structure while injecting subtype-complementary evidence via orthogonal accommodation.

⚙️ Method Details

From insight to implementation: the SCAN pipeline.

SCAN is a lightweight plug-and-play layer inserted after the backbone and before the unchanged GCD head. The three stages mirror expert diagnostic cognition while remaining fully differentiable.

Stage I

Predictive Suppression

The brain suppresses predictable sensory content, freeing capacity for unexpected signals. SCAN builds a sample-conditioned dominant anchor from evidence slots and geometrically projects out predictable components.

ri = xi − (xi⊤u)u

Residuals lie in the orthogonal complement, exposing what the dominant pattern cannot explain.

Stage II

Surprise-Evoked Salience

Residual magnitude measures image-conditional mismatch. A token is surprising when the dominant anchor explains it poorly. SCAN converts residual energy into salience weights and re-aggregates evidence weighted by surprise.

si = ‖ri‖2,   wi ∝ exp(si/τs)

Within-image ranking concentrates on coherent, unpredicted deviations without cross-dataset calibration.

Stage III

Complementary Accommodation

Discovery must integrate deviations without distorting known structure. SCAN constructs a novelty vector from excited evidence, projects it to the complementary subspace orthogonal to the dominant anchor, and injects it via a non-parametric surprise gate.

c̃ = c + γ · LN(Δ⊥ + xpool⊥)

The identity path recovers the original representation when γ is small, limiting feature drift.

📊 Experimental Results

Consistent gains across three discovery settings.

SCAN is evaluated on four biomedical datasets (GastroVision, HistoSet-5×14, MLL23, DERM12345) spanning endoscopy, histopathology, microscopy, and dermatology. Results show overall improvements in novel-disease discovery while revealing some dataset- and baseline-specific trade-offs.

🔌 Plug-and-Play Design

Insert SCAN into your GCD pipeline.

SCAN is a lightweight module between the vision backbone and any existing GCD head. It works with SimGCD, LegoGCD, SelEx, or other parametric/non-parametric frameworks without changing the original loss or clustering protocol.

Clinical Impact & Future Directions

GBD is not intended as a leaderboard against text-supervised multimodal models. It targets upstream clinical concept formation: organizing unlabeled archives into coherent cohorts that experts can verify, name, merge, or split before integration into guidelines and curated datasets. This matters because emerging diseases and subtypes often lack consistent nomenclature across sites and time, while clinicians require cohort-level discovery, representative prototypes, and similar-case lists rather than one-off predictions.

The three-setting benchmark and the cognition-inspired design offer a reusable interface for continual discovery, domain-shifted discovery, open-world recognition, and other tasks whose category inventory evolves over time.
